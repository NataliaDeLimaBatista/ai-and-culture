# Checklist for AI in Public Engagement and Cultural Projects

Use this checklist for community outreach, science communication, and cultural projects that involve AI.

- [ ] What social, cultural, educational, or communication problem will the project address?
- [ ] Who is the audience, and how will they participate in design and evaluation?
- [ ] Is AI necessary, or would a simpler approach meet the need?
- [ ] What texts, images, voices, works, data, or collections will the project use?
- [ ] Do permissions, licenses, or another appropriate basis cover those uses?
- [ ] Have people with relevant scientific, artistic, or cultural expertise reviewed the results?
- [ ] Have we clearly identified uncertainties, interpretations, and reconstructions alongside documented facts?
- [ ] Could the project reinforce stereotypes or misrepresent a group or community?
- [ ] Have we considered accessibility, linguistic diversity, and different literacy levels?
- [ ] How will we tell the audience where and how AI was used?
- [ ] Have we protected personal data, sensitive collections, and traditional knowledge?
- [ ] Who is responsible for the content, decisions, and any errors?
- [ ] Does the project acknowledge the work of the people involved?
- [ ] How will we document outcomes and lessons for evaluation and improvement?

Bring technical expertise, subject knowledge, and community participation together. Make decisions and responsibilities clear to the people the project serves.
